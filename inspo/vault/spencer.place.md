## What it is
The personal 'internet home' of Spencer Chang — artist, engineer and toymaker in San Francisco who makes 'technology that cultivates interdependence and human agency.' He is the maker behind the Wikipedia-multiplayer extension this bookmark is about, and the site itself is a living, collaboratively-editable room.

## What it shows
A dark, room-like homepage that opens 'Hi, I'm Spencer. Welcome to my internet home.' A live presence counter shows 'ppl(4)' currently visiting, and the page invites you to change the lights, move the furniture, or leave a note. Emoji-tagged nav (welcome 🚪 / describe 🟠 / collaborate 🌻 / create 💽 / write 🖨️ / support 🫶 / fits 🧢) leads to collections of his creations — playhtml 'tiny social networks', 'game for the internet', 'internet debris', computing shrines — plus a now-page, current reading and exhibitions.

## How it works
It's genuinely multiplayer: shared, persistent state (visitor count, movable furniture, lighting, notes) is powered by playhtml backed by a Cloudflare Worker ('sharingan'), so visitors edit the space in real time and the changes persist. You explore by scrolling the richly layered single page and clicking the emoji nav.

## How it's designed
A cozy, lo-fi 'soft internet' aesthetic: a charcoal/teal ground (rgb(47,51,54)) with cream text (rgb(235,232,227)) and a soft-yellow accent (rgb(234,234,111)), warm orange links. Playful display typography pairs Cooper and Vulf Sans with retro mono faces (Sono, VT323), and emoji double as navigation. Built on Astro + playhtml, image-heavy (46 imgs, 8 inline videos) to feel hand-furnished rather than templated.
